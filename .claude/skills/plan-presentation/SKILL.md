## Content Strategy

Before writing any React code, plan the presentation's narrative structure.
Content strategy principles adapted from the `presentation-maker`
marketplace skill (branded PPTX generation) and the `html-slide-builder`
skill (graphics-first HTML decks with inline SVG).

### Step 0: Understand the brief

If the brief is sparse or ambiguous, ask these before proceeding:

- **Audience:** Who will see this? What do they care about? Do they need to be convinced, or is this informational?
- **Intent:** What should the audience do after seeing this? Approve something? Fund work? Change direction?
- **Constraints:** How long is the presentation slot? Is this a leave-behind or a live presentation?
- **Sources:** Which documents are primary vs. supporting? Is the source material complete?

### Step 1: Choose a narrative posture

Pick ONE posture that shapes the tone of the entire deck:

| Posture | When to use | Tone |
|---------|------------|------|
| **Validation, then Extension** | Audience built the thing. You agree and want to build on it. | "Your work is strong. Here's what comes next." |
| **Problem, then Solution** | Audience is experiencing pain. You're proposing a fix. | "Here's the pain. Here's the path out." |
| **Comparison, then Recommendation** | Multiple options exist. You're helping them choose. | "Here are the tradeoffs. Here's what I'd pick." |
| **Education, then Implication** | Audience needs to understand before they can act. | "Here's how this works. Here's what it means for us." |
| **Status, then Direction** | Audience needs an update and a roadmap. | "Here's where we are. Here's where we're headed." |

### Step 2: Map the emotional arc

Every effective presentation follows this curve. Map each section to a beat:

1. **Attention** (1 section) — opening that makes the audience care
2. **Empathy** (1-2 sections) — show you understand their world
3. **Credibility** (2-3 sections) — demonstrate deep domain understanding
4. **Tension** (1-2 sections) — surface the unresolved problem or gap
5. **Resolution** (2-3 sections) — present the answer
6. **Call to Action** (1 section) — what happens next

Do not present the answer before the audience feels the problem.

### Step 3: Plan sections before coding

Produce a section outline with:

- **Section title** (5 words max)
- **Purpose** (one sentence: what does this section accomplish?)
- **Emotional beat** (which arc phase: attention, empathy, credibility, tension, resolution, CTA)
- **Primary visual** (what graphic, chart, or visual element carries this section?)
- **Evidence source** (which document or data point backs this?)

One idea per section. If a section has two unrelated points, split it.
